Seven mockups built back to back between April and June 2026, each one picking a different constraint on purpose. The bakery ran React and Vite against Sanity as a headless backend, with a separate studio holding singleton schemas for the hero, about, CTA and footer alongside product, category and testimonial documents. The first landscaper was React, Vite and Tailwind across eleven section components. The second answered the same brief with no build step at all — six hand-written HTML pages, one stylesheet, and `@vercel/analytics` as the only dependency. The HVAC page went the other way: Next 15 App Router, GSAP and Framer Motion, Lenis smooth scroll, a custom cursor, per-project detail routes, generated sitemap, robots and OG image. A coaching practice and a general business page are a single `index.html` each with no dependencies. The last put React Router in front of a Vercel serverless function posting through Resend, with a Playwright script that screenshots each build and a prerender pass over the static routes.

None of them shipped for a client. The point was finding out, before a client's money was on the line, which parts of a stack are load-bearing and which are habit — which is why the variance is in the tooling rather than the subject matter. Two of the seven use Tailwind, two use no build tooling at all, and one carries a CMS backend heavier than the site in front of it. That spread was the experiment.

Seven small sites built back to back, each one picking a different fight on purpose: a bakery on Sanity as a headless backend, two landscaping mockups testing how much a redesign should diverge from the original brief, an HVAC contractor page built around a long service list, a coaching practice, a general business template, and a straight utility page wired to Resend for its contact form. None of them shipped for a real client — they were reps.

The point was never a portfolio piece. It was finding out, before a client's money was on the line, which parts of a stack are load-bearing and which are habit. React and Vite showed up on most of them because the loop is fast and nothing about a five-page mockup needs a framework's opinions about routing or data fetching. Tailwind carried the styling everywhere it appeared, mostly because rebuilding a design system from scratch for a throwaway site is its own kind of waste.

What actually varied, deliberately, was scope: one site leaned on a CMS, one leaned on a component library, and one was built with almost no dependencies at all, to see what got harder without them.
